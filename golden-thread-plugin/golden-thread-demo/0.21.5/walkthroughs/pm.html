A product or project manager's first day

Someone who plans, tracks and reports rather than writes code. Plain words throughout. Practice project: demo-pizzabot (a pretend project; nothing here touches your real notes).

1. Pick the project up where it was left

What you are doing
Pick the project up where it was left
What to type
/gt:gt-open demo-pizzabot
What you will see
A short summary of PizzaBot 3000: what it is, what was decided, what is open, and a question about where to start.
Why it matters
You never re-read old notes to find your place; the summary is built from the project's own files.

2. Ask what to do first

What you are doing
Ask what to do first
What to type
/gt:gt-next
What you will see
Up to three lines: the one task to do now, why it is the one, and a command to try after it.
Why it matters
When everything feels urgent, one clear next step beats a list.

3. See what is waiting on you

What you are doing
See what is waiting on you
What to type
/gt:gt-list tasks mine
What you will see
Your open tasks, most urgent first, with due dates and anything waiting on someone.
Why it matters
Overdue and waiting items are the ones that slip; this shows them without opening a spreadsheet.

4. Add a task in one sentence

What you are doing
Add a task in one sentence
What to type
/gt:gt-create task Send the stakeholder update by Friday
What you will see
One new line under the project's Tasks, with the date understood from your words.
Why it matters
A task written now is a task not forgotten at 5 pm.

5. Write a decision down

What you are doing
Write a decision down
What to type
Write down a decision: we cap the negotiation at three rounds, because longer rounds lost customers
What you will see
A short decision record: what was decided, why, and what was rejected, filed in the project.
Why it matters
In three months nobody remembers why; the record does.

6. Find what the notes say

What you are doing
Find what the notes say
What to type
/gt:gt-query what did we decide about negotiation rounds
What you will see
The answer in a sentence or two, with the note it came from.
Why it matters
Your notes answer questions so you do not have to ask a colleague twice.

7. Close the day

What you are doing
Close the day
What to type
/gt:gt-close
What you will see
A plain brief of what moved, what was decided and what is blocked, then the notes saved and a handoff if anything is unfinished.
Why it matters
Tomorrow's session starts from what you learned today, not from a blank page.
