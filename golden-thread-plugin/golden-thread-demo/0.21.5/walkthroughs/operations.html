An operations engineer's first day

Someone who runs systems and changes them carefully. Practice project: demo-pizzabot (a pretend project; nothing here touches your real notes).

1. Open the runbook's project

What you are doing
Open the runbook's project
What to type
/gt:gt-open demo-pizzabot
What you will see
A summary of the project, its runbook, recent incidents and open tasks.
Why it matters
You start from the written state of the system, not from memory.

2. See what is overdue

What you are doing
See what is overdue
What to type
/gt:gt-list tasks overdue
What you will see
Overdue tasks with how many days late and who is waiting.
Why it matters
Late rotations and renewals show up before they bite.

3. Check a claim before you act

What you are doing
Check a claim before you act
What to type
/gt:gt-validate the certificate on the order service expires on 2026-12-01
What you will see
A fresh reviewer checks the claim against the rules and the artifact and reports confirmed, refuted or unknown.
Why it matters
A number that matters is verified by something that did not make it up.

4. Write the steps down while they are fresh

What you are doing
Write the steps down while they are fresh
What to type
/gt:gt-work
What you will see
The commands, gotchas and the incident summary go to the runbook and the project's findings.
Why it matters
The next on-call person finds the fix instead of rediscovering it.

5. Leave a handoff

What you are doing
Leave a handoff
What to type
/gt:gt-create handoff
What you will see
A handoff note: what is done, what is half-done, and what to verify first.
Why it matters
Whoever picks this up knows what is true and what is only believed.

6. Close the session

What you are doing
Close the session
What to type
/gt:gt-close
What you will see
The notes saved, tasks updated and the session's claim on the vault released.
Why it matters
Nothing is left half-written and nobody else is blocked.
