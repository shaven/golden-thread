An executive's first day

Someone who decides and delegates. Short answers, decisions first. Practice project: demo-pizzabot (a pretend project; nothing here touches your real notes).

1. Get the picture of one project

What you are doing
Get the picture of one project
What to type
/gt:gt-open demo-pizzabot
What you will see
A short summary with the status, the risks and the decisions waiting for you.
Why it matters
You get the state of play in under a minute.

2. Ask what needs you

What you are doing
Ask what needs you
What to type
/gt:gt-next
What you will see
Up to three lines: the one thing that needs you now, why, and what to try after.
Why it matters
Your time goes to the decision that is actually waiting.

3. Work through what is waiting

What you are doing
Work through what is waiting
What to type
/gt:gt-handle task
What you will see
One waiting item at a time with the choices: close it, keep it, drop it or push it to a date.
Why it matters
A backlog gets cleared in minutes instead of growing.

4. Sort what you jotted down

What you are doing
Sort what you jotted down
What to type
/gt:gt-review
What you will see
Each loose note from your inbox, with the project it probably belongs to, for you to accept or change.
Why it matters
Thoughts caught in passing end up where someone will act on them.

5. Ask your notes a question

What you are doing
Ask your notes a question
What to type
/gt:gt-query what is the biggest open risk
What you will see
A short answer with the note it came from.
Why it matters
You ask in your own words; nobody has to prepare a report.

6. Close the day

What you are doing
Close the day
What to type
/gt:gt-close
What you will see
A brief first: what moved, what was decided, what is blocked, then the saved notes.
Why it matters
The record of today exists without you writing it.
